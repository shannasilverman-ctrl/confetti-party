# Confetti product and brand direction

## Product feeling

Confetti is the calm co-host that turns a half-formed idea into a gathering the
host can actually enjoy.

The experience should feel:

- warm enough for birthdays, holidays, Shabbat, and family rituals;
- polished enough for weddings and milestone events;
- playful without becoming childish;
- useful before, during, and after the gathering;
- authored and human, never like a generic dashboard template.

## Chosen brand direction: Editorial Spark

Three directions were compared:

| Direction       | Distinctive small mark | Broad event range | Warmth | Premium feel | Product fit |
| --------------- | ---------------------: | ----------------: | -----: | -----------: | ----------: |
| Editorial Spark |                      5 |                 5 |      4 |            5 |           5 |
| Party Hands     |                      4 |                 3 |      5 |            3 |           4 |
| Modern Keepsake |                      4 |                 4 |      3 |            5 |           3 |

Editorial Spark is the primary system. Party Hands can remain an illustration
language inside celebratory moments. Modern Keepsake can inform wedding and
holiday theme packs without becoming the master brand.

### The mark

The open C means a plan does not need to be complete before someone can begin.
The spark marks the moment an idea becomes a gathering.

The mark must:

- remain recognizable at favicon size;
- work in one color over photography;
- use the coral spark when color is available;
- avoid a generic gradient app-tile treatment;
- appear with the editorial wordmark in navigation and key trust moments.

## Visual grammar

- **Canvas:** pale lilac paper, not sterile white.
- **Ink:** deep aubergine for warmth and authority.
- **Action:** accessible coral/plum, with the vivid coral reserved for sparks,
  gradients, and celebration.
- **Type:** Fraunces for emotional promises and event moments; Outfit for
  practical planning.
- **Photography:** people in the middle of the gathering, not styled objects in
  empty rooms.
- **Shape:** large editorial frames and soft sheets; rounded cards only when
  they contain a real task or object.
- **Motion:** one meaningful burst after completion, plus restrained micro
  celebration around the brand mark.

## Page hierarchy

Every public surface should answer these questions in order:

1. What relief does Confetti create?
2. What is the one action I should take?
3. Can I see a real product outcome?
4. Why should I trust it with my gathering?

The landing page uses one dominant promise and one dominant CTA. The sample
invite is the proof surface. Detailed product moments appear as an authored
journey rather than a flat feature grid.

## Signature product moment: Party Booth

Party Booth is part of the event experience, not a photo-hosting product:

1. The host opens the invite/share dialog and prints or shares the booth QR.
2. A guest scans directly into the event-specific booth.
3. The guest takes or chooses a photo.
4. Confetti renders the event name, date, theme palette, and selected frame
   locally in the browser.
5. The guest saves or shares the finished image from their phone.

The original and finished image never need to leave the guest's device. The
interface must say this before a guest chooses a photo and again before saving.

## Release scorecard

Before promoting a visual change, review desktop and mobile against:

- one clear action in the first viewport;
- mark recognizable at 16, 32, and 64 pixels;
- no generic blank-card section after the hero;
- event photography and product proof feel connected;
- consistent typography across landing, workspace, invite, and booth;
- comfortable 44-pixel touch targets;
- no clipped text or horizontal overflow at 320 pixels;
- AA contrast for body text and controls;
- reduced-motion support;
- the host can begin with blanks and return later;
- the guest can understand the invite without creating an account.
